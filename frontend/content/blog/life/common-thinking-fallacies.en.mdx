Hello everyone, welcome to Cold-Knowledge Blind Box. The last few episodes were NVIDIA's early days. This time we rest and talk about spotting and cracking everyday logic fallacies.

One. Why does learning to spot them matter? Because a lot of fallacies hide well.

Hard to notice.

The old line:

"Sold, and still counting the money for them."

But if we learn to see them —

the upsides are huge.

- Shopping: dodge merchant tricks, save a pile of stupid money.

- Work: sharper judgment, grab the core of the problem, climb faster.

- Decisions: fewer detours, see through the surface.

- Even online fights: spot the hole in the other side's logic, hit from a higher dimension, take the high ground.

- And stay awake in life, so every call, every decision, is more solid.

Two. The straw man.

Fallacies hide, so missing them hurts.

First:

— the straw man.

What is it?

What's the harm?

How does it move us?

[Definition]

A straw man is fabricating a "fake view" that looks like the original, then knocking it down.

Can't beat the real person, so you build a scarecrow and beat that.

It works by:

- twisting the meaning

- exaggerating, even inventing, the other side's view

then attacking.

A classic thinking error.

[Common?]

Life, the net, everywhere.

[Example 1]

- A: We should cut carbon.

- B: So you want to stop all industry and go back to the Stone Age?

[Example 2]

- Parent: Play less, leave more time for study.

- Kid: You just want me to study until I drop dead?

[Example 3]

- Colleague A: Add a bit of free discussion in meetings so people can actually talk.

- Colleague B: You want us sitting in meetings all day doing nothing else?

[Example 4]

- Someone says build high-speed rail.

  Someone answers: you want to waste all our money on bureaucrats?

- Someone says build schools and hospitals.

  Someone answers: education is already a rat race, why not reform first?

- Praise an iPhone, get tagged "worshipping the West."

  Praise a Huawei phone, get tagged "dumb leek."

[Harm]

The straw man turns talk into mutual attack.

- You talk facts, they talk camp.

- You talk reason, they talk feeling.

- You talk a general pattern, they talk one case.

Their point was never to reason.

It was to twist and inflate, heat up your view, move the focus, build a scarecrow,

"beat" it,

and feel like they won.

Next time you meet that person —

throw them this episode.

Three. The red herring.

Second:

— the red herring.

What is it?

How is it different from a straw man?

[Story]

A red herring is a smoked fish with a strong smell.

In police-dog training they throw that smell to pull the dog off the original trail.

[Definition]

A red herring is —

using a topic that "looks related, isn't"

to move attention.

[Example]

- A: I adopted a stray yesterday.

- B: Then why aren't you at the nursing home taking care of old people?

[Versus straw man]

Same: both dodge the real question.

Different:

- Straw man — twist their meaning, then attack.

- Red herring — bring in a new topic, cover the focus.

[More]

- Parent: Why did your grades drop?

  Kid: Some classmates don't even use phones and their grades dropped too!

- Netizen: The country should spend more on education.

  Reply: You don't love the country! You want to cut defense and wait for invasion?

Politics loves this.

A politician throws a red herring, the crowd's attention runs off,

the fight upgrades, the original topic is forgotten.
