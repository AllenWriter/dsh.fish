Hello everyone, welcome to Cold-Knowledge Blind Box. Last time: how Trump, with lawyer Roy Cohn, won the racial-discrimination case against the Trump Organization. Roy was Trump's lawyer and his first business mentor. He taught almost the creed that ran Trump's life:

1. First: attack, attack, keep attacking.

Cohn sued the plaintiffs first, said the city was persecuting capitalists, asked for a hundred million in damages. Impossible, but scrambling the other side's formation matters.

Never think you are wrong. Find the moment, reverse the rake, make them chaos.

1. Second: admit nothing, deny everything.

Roy grabbed the lack of hard evidence, tangled in court, never admitted.

1. Find the key of the problem.

The Godfather line: a person who sees the essence in half a second, and a person who never sees it in a lifetime, are fated differently.

Roy found the person behind the suit, Walter sitting below the court. It was Justice that made New York sue the group. Roy knew they could not win in court, so blackmailing a withdrawal with an affair was already the best ending.

Those three, Trump's creed. Attack Trump, attack back. Full of information, chin high at the enemy.

After the first fight he stood firm in the family group. Unlike dad, who made public housing for city poor who could not buy, Trump wanted the metropolis. He wanted the faded Commodore near Grand Central. A big redo. On paper a bottom-fishing chance: New York center, once a famous hotel.

The 70s: the city near bankrupt, crime off the chart, everyone waiting to be saved. A Gotham feel. Other states would not help. The mayor had to find a way. Save the economy, stop unemployment. Partner with developers.

Family still said no. Think: in a downturn, still pouring money into a luxury hotel. Hard. Trump proving himself, matching father. It clicked. Shocked the country: 40 years tax-free. Saved hundreds of millions.

He did not name it Trump Hotel then. Hyatt. The fit-out was palatial, very luxury. After completion it was New York news. Reporters never stopped. A new landmark, no question.

Around then Trump slowly realized: I need to be famous. Unlike low-key dad, he was loud. Found love, a Czech model, a female Trump. She ran hotel interiors, her own jewelry brand. Three kids. Almost the life most people envy.

How to get famous: prime-time TV, and say shocking things on it. Lifestyles of the Rich and Famous. Buy a corner of Fifth Avenue. He did. Today's Trump Tower. Half apartments, half hotel.

Ambition other people have, I don't. After the tower, he put work into the marketing gimmick. Rich people, celebrities buy here. Even the Queen of England will come. He knew it was a boast. Media only prints clickbait. Each takes what they need. In the 80s Trump already knew to bind a house to a dream and a luxury good. Am I selling you a house? I am selling the chance to be someone in New York. Opening prices were high: two-bed 1.1 million, penthouse 11 million.

He also got a government tax cut. Smarter: Trump Tower, only the name is Trump's. The rooms were already sold. He kept management and one penthouse.

He could not sit still. He also

built casinos, an airline, held events. Miss America, TV. PR, marketing, not good at management. Hidden trouble.

His style is high leverage. Three hotels in a row, different investors each. People warned: the three compete with each other, not necessarily good for you. When the last, Taj Mahal, opened, plenty of trouble. Eight hundred million short of finishing. Borrowed everywhere, even 14% interest. Finally done, hit the 1987 crash. Many millionaires went bankrupt on the spot. Even fewer came to the casino.

Three families' money. Taj Mahal, opening crisis, finance manager warning. 1987 crash.

At the worst, love took a hit too. Ivana the power woman, for a moment outshone him. He cheated, got caught. Paid 25 million to split. Now he was really bankrupt.

He told the creditors who came to the door,

bankruptcy reorganization, debt to equity. Keep management, keep the name. If I truly lie down, nobody gets their money. Better each step back. Don't chase so hard, I'll find a way to earn again. When it is good later, this Trump will pay what is owed. He did not run. The company later listed.

On TV before, the host

joked, no money, become president. The turn came in 1999. A friend, a wrestler named Jesse, with zero political experience, became governor of Minnesota. Trump thought about politics, did not want people to guess.

So every so often he jumped out with a wild take, then announced he was out of the race. That is Trump. Better be cursed than unknown.

When other politicians mocked him and dropped their guard, in 2016 he won the 45th presidency.

Probe, wait for chance, watch the crowd. 45th president.
