If your job vanished from the world one day, what would happen? Statistics say 40% of people think their work contributes nothing. If it disappeared, still nothing. A terrible job. Is your job a pile of shit? Why do bosses pay people to do meaningless work? Today we talk about bullshit jobs.

In 1930 the economist Keynes predicted that, with tech, efficiency would soar. In the 21st century people would work three hours a day on average. We know that prophecy failed. We work at least eight. Companies that need no overtime are rare. Tech keeps advancing. Why has work time not shrunk?

Anthropology professor David Graeber wrote in 2013: because society is full of garbage jobs. Extremely inefficient, little social contribution, yet people do them day after day to live. The piece hit hard. He got bullshit-job stories from around the world. Later he put the leaks from every industry into the book Bullshit Jobs.

A bullshit job is one most people think the world would be fine without, but you have a family to feed, so you must act as if it is important and necessary. Some people say work does not need to mean anything; the point is money. After many shares, the author noticed a pattern: reporters, nurses, firefighters may not earn a lot, but they generally feel the work means something. Another group: I earn a lot, sit in AC all day, most of the work is pretending to work. I live in pain every day. I need reasons to get myself out of bed.

As I read it, the author splits these jobs into five types. First: flunkies. The point is to set off someone else's status, make them look important. You could handle it yourself, but you still have her take the call and transfer it, so the company looks bigger. Other people feel stronger because they gained a subordinate.

Second: goons. Main job is to grow the employer's interest, with no real help to society. Corporate counsel complain they help the company dodge tax or attack rivals.

Third: duct tapers? The book calls them box tickers. The work is surface effort, so the company looks like it is doing something: stacks of PPT, yearly goals, plans, reports to apply for money, or house magazines nobody reads.

Fourth: taskmasters, people whose job is to create bullshit tasks for subordinates. A manager admits they invent metrics and forms so staff look busy. Without the paperwork, everyone's work would go fine.

Last: duct tapers in another sense — the book also has duct tapers who patch what the company knows is broken and will not fix. Many customer-service people have no real power over the product. They can only answer officially, a punching bag for customers.

Those five. Maybe you saw yourself. Besides them, the author finds a disaster zone: finance. From the 1950s to the 1980s, productivity and wages in the West rose together: higher productivity, higher average pay. After 1980, productivity kept rising year by year, average wages did not. The author says capitalism turned financial. A lot of finance appeared, and finance is the ultimate bullshit workplace.

Banks control huge money through deposits, then allocate it to subordinates, and that is power. To control more subordinates, more power, they create unnecessary work. Money has long gone to laundering havens, but to keep every approval from looking fake, the bank hires someone else to fake data so it looks as if they really caught illegal funds. A risk manager, once they see it, knows 80% of bank posts could be automated. The boss will not, because without those staff the boss cannot exercise power.

Most jobs seen as bullshit did not exist in the past. Why the surge? A big reason is a shift in ideas. Pay used to be measured by "labor product" — how much you dug, how many pots you made. After the industrial revolution the measure became "time," the hour system. Hard to imagine before. When the boss buys your time, of course they want you working the whole time. Hard workers cannot leave early; they get more work. Often there is not that much to do, so people pretend, or add work that looks like work and produces nothing. Efficiency tanks. U.S. office workers spend under 40% of time on actual work; the rest is meetings, email, admin trivia. Many admit they do other things on the clock, wage thieves.

The author also notes: even if you are used to all-day meetings or fooling around, bullshit work or wage-theft still hits body and mind. In 1901 German psychologist Karl Groos found that when an infant first sees their action have a predictable effect on the world, whatever the effect, they look very happy.

That psychology is a sense of efficacy, a human need. Simply: people need to feel they are doing something that means something. Do meaningless things every day and you suffer. Many people in bullshit jobs feel mental pain and pressure because they never get to feel meaning and achievement.

So why not quit and do something that means more? First, many need the job to feed a family. Even if the content is empty, they cannot lose the income. Second, social and cultural pressure makes it hard to leave. Unemployment feels shameful. Society's definition of success often hangs on title and pay. People fear looking unsuccessful, so they endure pain rather than risk joblessness.

Company structure also feeds bullshit jobs. Many firms use hierarchy, control and supervision, which creates piles of admin. To keep the structure they invent meaningless posts to fill the layers. Staff are forced into empty tasks because that is how the company runs.

How to deal? First, try to find some meaningful slice in the work, raise your sense of efficacy. Even if most of the day is empty, try to do something that means something in the limited time, or find a project that feels like achievement.

Second, consider changing the career path, look for more meaningful work. If the current job hurts, raise skills, look at other chances. That takes courage. Sometimes change is necessary.

Finally, at the social and cultural level, we need to look again at what work is. Work should not only be money. It should also bring meaning and achievement. Society and firms should care about psychological need, cut unnecessary layers, encourage more meaningful content.

Bullshit jobs are common. We are not completely powerless. Personal effort and social reform can slowly cut their number, so more people find meaning and achievement at work.
