Hello everyone, welcome to Cold-Knowledge Blind Box. Last time: the Dutch East India Company, the world's first listed company. Humanity got the key to the modern capital-market box. Globalization, yes. And it also summoned the beast at the bottom of human nature: speculation.

Bubbles and crises come with it. The leek-cutting tricks of modern finance — shorting, insider trades, pumping a stock to dump it — already have their baby versions in the seventeenth century.

This episode: once you have the first stock exchange, how does the world flip over.

VOC's business is simple. Raise money, build ships, sail. When a ship comes home, dump the goods on the market, subtract cost, that's profit. One ship back and you print money. Routes are the usual routes. Once you set a departure, you can roughly time the return. If the ship comes back, you will make a killing.

The most common modern-market move is fake news to move the tape.

Suppose you want to buy a lot, but not at a high price. What do you do?

Start a rumor the ship hit a disaster and isn't coming back. Bare mouth isn't enough. No newspapers, no social media. How do you put the story out? You need the bottom-layer password of human nature: everyone loves gossip. Two hundred meters from the Amsterdam exchange is the famous red-light district, originally built to serve sailors on long voyages. That's also where news is fastest.

A common routine: invent an English sailor. Rounding the Cape he saw a storm-smashed hull aground. You could smell the spices for miles. A ragged VOC flag. The story doesn't need to be precise. It needs a nose and eyes. Then the rumor stands up. Once the chain reaction starts, shareholders who don't know what's going on dump like crazy. Price crashes. You buy a pile.

That little trick is still around. Next, a more advanced play: the short.

Among VOC founding shareholders is Isaac Le Maire. We'll call him Brother Le. Biggest individual holder, 2.2%. He isn't clean. Caught faking numbers in the cargo, kicked out of management. A lot of "innovation" in business starts as someone who just can't swallow it. Brother Le wants to mess with VOC, and make money while messing. He stares at all those shares and a wild idea is born.

I put my whole fortune up as collateral and write other shareholders a note. You lend me stock. At expiry I return the stock, plus interest. Black and white. If I lie I'm a dog.

One person isn't enough. He uses connections, other rich merchants: let's borrow every share we can, sell them together at a high price, make a trend. When the price drops, we buy back. An insider short club. Selling isn't enough. He wants fake news with it.

Henri IV of France has wanted a piece of VOC for a long time, his own East India company, a share of the great voyages. Same routes. A new competitor would squeeze Dutch business. Brother Le goes to France, comes home, and circulates his chat with the king. The stock drops 13% on cue. Just as the club is about to harvest, a variable. The story gets so big the Dutch government decides to take a stake. A rescue. Sound familiar?

The government doesn't put in cash. It puts in policy. One: limit shorts. Also: right to wage war, try criminals, negotiate with foreigners, build forts, found colonies, issue its own money.

In effect VOC is the Dutch state, scooping huge wealth. All that money moving also founds the Bank of Amsterdam, Europe's financial center. Any European can deposit and borrow.

Then people get giddy. The Dutch don't feel their interests and the state's are the same. During the Eighty Years' War with Spain, Spain can still borrow seamlessly from Dutch banks. Even with the money, Spain still gets flattened. Dutch merchants smirk: see, capital has no borders, and we still win.

Then the English come. Four wars, large and small, over who monopolizes the routes. To fund the fight, the English offer above-market interest at Dutch banks and issue ultra-high-yield debt. Every time an English ship sinks a VOC merchantman, VOC shareholders have already handed their savings to the English.

When the war is won, England can simply refuse the interest. What can Holland do? Europe's financial center becomes London. VOC dies from there.
