Hello everyone, welcome to Cold-Knowledge Blind Box. If you ask who will move the world most in the next four years, it has to be the man just elected the next U.S. president: Donald Trump.

The best campaign line I ever heard about his legend goes like this: the second a bullet is about to hit his skull, he seems to hear God whisper. He tilts an ear, hunting for the source. Fate tugs the thread so gently that the killing shot only grazes him. Then he finally makes out what God said: this is a vote for you.

The man who lost four years ago is back on a red wave that swept both houses. He is not only returning to the White House in 2025; he took majorities in the Senate and the House. In a country of separated powers, that is the legislature and the executive in one grab.

This episode is a little gossip. What kind of house did Trump grow up in? How does a rich kid who looks like he will only inherit the firm grow into a businessman who stretches the family map sixty times, and then onto the peak of American power?

The story starts with grandpa. Frederick Trump is born in 1869 in Kallstadt, Germany. For ease, we'll call him Grandpa T. The family is drowning in debt. At fourteen he is sent to learn barbering, and he quickly sees the trade is already full in a small town.

To dodge conscription and hunt a better shot, he emigrates to America in 1885, joining a sister already settled there. He starts a small business, first a barbershop. So-so. No fortune, just rice in the bowl.

The window opens fast: the western gold rush. People pour in, demand pours in. Grandpa T already has a basic commercial nose. He pivots, heads west, opens restaurants and taverns. Rooms, too, and a little entertainment on the side. Those businesses pile up the family's first money, the stone under everything later.

By the time Dad T — Fred Trump — inherits, it is the 1920s. The family has a little money, not a lot. Dad T knows the wants of the urban poor and the lower middle class. He founds a low-income housing company. Then the Depression hits, everything wilts. Brooklyn and Queens are not the developed places they are now. They are still full of city laborers and ruined office workers.

Dad T puts money into a lot of affordable housing. He can stay close to government, collect subsidies, and look charitable in public. Emperor Qin stepping on a live wire — jackpot. The Trumps become the local name in development. By the 1960s his company has built more than 27,000 low-income apartments and row houses.

Success or not, Dad T's firm is also investigated for allegedly gaming Federal Housing Administration rules. He is accused of racial discrimination in rentals, which does not help his name in some neighborhoods. That plants a seed for the younger Trump's later education.

That decisive bet keeps the family business standing, and stacks wealth and connections in New York. Still, this is a comfortably rich family, not the same weight class as the all-American names — Kennedys, Fords, Rockefellers.

1946. Our Trump is born. Fourth child: an older brother, two older sisters, a younger brother. That many siblings in a fairly rich house — of course there will be a succession drama.

Dad T is especially fond of the eldest, Fred Trump Jr. You can see it in the name. Same as dad, just a "junior" in front. The plan is to pass the firm to the first son.

Expectations for Donald are not high. Maybe that is exactly why the two Trumps walk such different roads.
