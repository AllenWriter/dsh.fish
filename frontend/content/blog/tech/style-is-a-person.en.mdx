## Label styles by writer

Someone asked me to sort drafts into narrative, how-to, and opinion. That was not usable. I started labeling styles by writer instead. One tells history as a business war. One teaches by asking "and then." One dissects an organization under the chapter titles of a military classic. When I mix, I name the move I am borrowing. I do not impersonate a whole person.

## A borrowed skeleton goes hollow

I tried wrapping a finished airline story in someone else's chapter skeleton. Swifts, intent, positioning, campaign. The structure was tidy. The result was worse. The original had already explained a war of perception. Another layer of volume titles was like rewriting the menu for a dish that was already seasoned. The reader still ate the same food. They just tripped on the menu first.

## Named moves, not a costume

What you can borrow is a move: a number, a follow-up question, a collision of two weights in the opening. What you cannot borrow is a whole skeleton. That skeleton grew out of their material. Change the material and the bones go hollow. Hollow structure is noisier than no structure.

So a style library is not a costume shop. It is a list of named moves. Use it too whole and the piece feels fake. Use it too shattered and you are only showing off. The usable middle is found by trying it on a real draft, not by imagining it against a taxonomy.
